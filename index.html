Know what site work will cost before your due-diligence window closes.

A parcel-specific estimate for driveway, grading, septic feasibility, and utility distance on raw land in Tennessee — built from lidar elevation data and soil survey data for the actual parcel, not a county average.

The problem

You're inside a due-diligence window on a parcel and you don't know whether making it buildable — driveway, clearing, grading a pad, septic, getting utilities to the site — costs $20,000 or $120,000. State-average guidance doesn't answer that. A site engineer or surveyor can, but hiring one is slow and expensive relative to a 30–90 day window, especially for a single-lot residential buyer.

What the report covers

  • Driveway length and grade from the road to a buildable bench
  • Cut-and-fill volume estimate for a house pad
  • Septic feasibility class, from soil survey data
  • Distance to the nearest utility line
  • Floodplain / wetland flag
  • A plain-English cost range with the two or three biggest cost drivers explained

Tennessee parcels only, for now. This is a screening-grade estimate meant to help you decide whether to keep negotiating or walk away — not a substitute for a licensed surveyor, engineer, or contractor bid. Generated from public elevation and soil data (USGS, NRCS) using automated measurement plus an AI-written summary of the numbers. Not an appraisal, not a perc test, not a guarantee.
