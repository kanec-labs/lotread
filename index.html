The problem

You're inside a due-diligence window on a parcel and you don't know whether making it buildable — driveway, clearing, grading a pad, septic, getting utilities to the site — costs $20,000 or $120,000. State-average guidance doesn't answer that. A site engineer or surveyor can, but hiring one is slow and expensive relative to a 30–90 day window, especially for a single-lot residential buyer.

What the report covers

  • Driveway length and grade from the road to a buildable bench
  • Cut-and-fill volume estimate for a house pad
  • Septic feasibility class, from soil survey data
  • Distance to the nearest utility line
  • Floodplain / wetland flag
  • A plain-English cost range with the two or three biggest cost drivers explained

This is a screening-grade estimate meant to help you decide whether to keep negotiating or walk away — not a substitute for a licensed surveyor, engineer, or contractor bid. Generated from public elevation and soil data (USGS, NRCS) using automated measurement plus an AI-written summary of the numbers. Not an appraisal, not a perc test, not a guarantee.

Questions

Is my lot buildable?

Most raw land is buildable to some degree — the real question is what it costs to get there. LotRead's report gives you a parcel-specific site-work cost range and flags issues like floodplain, wetland, or poor septic feasibility that can rule a lot out or change the math entirely.

How much does site work cost on raw land?

It depends entirely on the parcel. Driveway length and grade, cut-and-fill volume, septic feasibility, and utility distance can push a single lot's site-work cost anywhere from $20,000 to $120,000 or more. LotRead's report gives you a specific range for your parcel instead of a guess.

What does a LotRead report include?

A LotRead report covers driveway length and grade, cut-and-fill volume for a house pad, septic feasibility class, distance to the nearest utility line, and a floodplain/wetland flag, summarized into a plain-English cost range. It's a screening-grade estimate, not a substitute for a licensed surveyor, engineer, or contractor bid, generated from public elevation and soil data using automated measurement plus an AI-written summary of the numbers. See the sample report for what this looks like on an actual parcel.

How is this different from state-average cost guides?

State-average guides tell you what site work tends to cost somewhere in your state — not what it costs on your specific parcel. LotRead is built from lidar elevation data and soil survey data for the actual lot, not a statewide or county average.
